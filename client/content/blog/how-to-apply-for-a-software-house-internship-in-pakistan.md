Apply between March and May for a summer place, send a one page CV with a link somebody can click, and use the company's own application form if it has one. ## The summer window closes earlier than you think

Students apply when their exams finish. Companies decide who they are taking weeks before that. Those two facts are the whole problem.

| When | Inside the company | What you should be doing |
| --- | --- | --- |
| March to April | Summer intake gets settled | Apply. This is the window |
| May | Shortlists closing, some places still moving | Apply, and follow up once |
| June | Applications arrive in a flood | Apply anyway, expect silence |
| July to August | Interns onboarded and being supervised | Ask about an off season block |
| December to January | A small winter intake, few applicants | Underrated. Apply |

Do not wait for an advertised vacancy either. Plenty of software houses here never post one, and take interns because somebody asked at a moment when there was work to hand out.

## Where to send it, in order of what works

1. **The company's own form or careers page.** If it exists, use it and only it. Emailing around a form signals that you did not look.
2. **A careers or HR address published on the site.** Not the sales address, and not a personal Gmail you found somewhere.
3. **LinkedIn.** Apply to the actual post. A connection request with two lines of context is fine; ten identical ones are not.
4. **A referral.** A senior who works there, a teacher who knows the founder. The strongest route by far, and the one students under use because it feels like cheating. It is not, it is how most hiring works.

Never send one email addressed to twelve companies. It is the easiest thing in the world to spot and to delete.

## A one page CV when you have no work history

The blank experience section is what makes people panic, and panic produces a page of adjectives. Reorder the page so what is true about you comes first.

| Section | What goes in it | The common mistake |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Header | Name, city, phone with country code, email, GitHub link | CNIC, age, marital status |
| Summary line | Your degree, your year, the one track you want | "Seeking a challenging position in a reputed organisation" |
| Projects | Two or three, each with a stack, a sentence, a link | The class wide semester project, unlinked |
| Skills | Only things you have used, grouped by kind | Twenty items you have merely heard of |
| Education | Degree, university, expected year, CGPA if it helps | Matric and FSc subject lists |
| The rest | Courses with certificates, a competition, a society role | "References available on request" |

Projects go above education, because education is the one thing every other applicant also has. One page, exported as PDF, named `ayesha-siddiqui-cv.pdf` rather than `cv final final 2.pdf`. A photo is not disqualifying here, but it costs space you do not have.

Be strict about skills: assume you will be asked to explain anything you list by someone who uses it daily. Two you can defend beat eight you cannot.

## A GitHub link beats every adjective on the page

Whoever reads your CV gives it under a minute. In that minute, "passionate, hardworking, quick learner" carries no information, because no CV anywhere says the opposite. A link is checkable, and being willing to be checked is the signal.

What makes a repository worth linking:

- **A README saying what it is, what it uses, and how to run it.** Most student repos have none, so three paragraphs puts you ahead of the pile.
- **It runs.** Somebody may clone it, and they will not ask you for the undocumented variable it needs to start.
- **Commits spread across days**, not one called "initial commit" holding the whole project.
- **A live URL** if it can be deployed, and no `node_modules` or real API keys in the history.

Two small finished projects beat one abandoned ambitious one. A to do API with authentication, tests and a README says more than a half built social network, because the boring one proves you can finish.

If you have nothing to link, that is a weekend problem, not a career problem. Our [free courses](/courses) will get you the ground work, the [full stack web track](/learn/full-stack-web) included, but they end in an exam and a certificate, not a portfolio piece. The thing you put on the CV has to be something you built yourself afterwards, and you have to be able to talk about the parts you wrote rather than the parts you copied.

## The email

Five things belong in it: which track, one line about why this company, what you have built, when you are available, and a PDF CV attached. The rest is noise.

```
Subject: Internship application: Frontend, June to July, Ayesha Siddiqui

Hello,

I am a final year Computer Science student at [university] in
Islamabad, applying for a frontend internship. I am free from 1 June
and can work full time until 31 July.

Your team builds mostly in React and Next.js, which is what I have
been working in. The most relevant thing I have made is a bus
timetable site my department uses to check routes before class:
github.com/ayeshas/campus-timetable

My CV is attached, one page. If a short task is part of your process,
I am happy to do one.

Thank you for your time,
Ayesha Siddiqui
+92 300 1234567
```

Leave out your matric percentage, the paragraph hoping the email finds the reader in the best of health, a folder of six certificates, and any mention of pay. The right moment to ask about money is when somebody has said they want you.

One practical thing: a scanned CV can run to several megabytes, and large attachments get stripped or bounced by some mail systems. Compress it first, with [our PDF compressor](/tools/pdf-compressor) or anything else. A CV that never arrived looks the same as no CV at all.

## What happens after you send it

It lands in a shared inbox or a list of form submissions. Somebody, usually an engineer rather than a recruiter, skims it in a batch with thirty others, often weeks later. If it survives, you get a short call to check you are real and free on the dates you claimed. Many companies then send a small timeboxed task: build one page, fix one bug, write one script. Then a conversation about your project, where the question is almost always "why did you do it that way".

From send to answer is anywhere between three days and a month. The most common outcome, by a wide margin, is nothing.

## Why most applications are ignored

The silence is usually structural rather than a verdict on you.

- **There is no slot.** The most common reason by far. A team of twelve can supervise two interns, not twenty.
- **No CV attached**, or a Drive link demanding an access request. Nobody is requesting access.
- **The CV carries no evidence**, only self description.
- **The track does not exist there.** A company building ERP systems will not invent a game development internship.
- **The dates do not work.** You want two weeks in August, they run monthly blocks from June.
- **The wrong company name is in the greeting.** It ends the review instantly.

One more, and it is uncomfortable. At most small software houses, reading intern applications is unpaid overhead squeezed between client deliverables, done quickly by someone who would rather be writing code. Good applicants get missed. That is not a reason to give up, it is a reason to make the first ten seconds do real work, and to apply to six companies properly rather than sixty carelessly.

Follow up once, about ten days later, in the same thread, three lines long. Then let it go and write the next one.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### When is the best time to apply for summer software internships in Pakistan?
The ideal application window is between March and May. Most software houses in Islamabad, Lahore, and Karachi finalize their summer intern shortlists before university final semester exams conclude in June.

### What should a Computer Science student put on a CV with no work experience?
Lead with practical coding projects (each accompanied by a public GitHub link and tech stack summary), followed by verifiable technical skills, online course certificates with credential IDs, and university education. Keep the resume strictly to one page.

### Do software houses in Pakistan prefer paid or unpaid internships?
Both exist. Established multinational IT companies offer paid summer internships with stipends through formal recruitment drives, while many growing software houses offer unpaid training-focused internships with real project experience and recommendation letters.

### How many companies should I apply to for an internship?
Focus on tailoring 8 to 15 thoughtful applications with company-specific project mentions rather than mass-emailing generic resumes to 80 software houses. Tailored emails with working GitHub repos have a significantly higher response rate.
